Tab bar: medium tint (1.9 on TestFlight, build 166)

Pick a holiday; the tab selection moves on its own so you can see the pill slide, the colours drift and the glow trail. All 21 holidays and commemorations from holidays.json are in the floating bar, in calendar order from Halloween; hovering a pill shows its date.

Receipt, what's real: the order screen, colours and button style are the app's (OrdersView.swift's OrderPill, Theme.swift). The receipt is filled from what the app already keeps for each order: brand, items, quantities, subtotal, shipping, discounts, tax, total, order number, date and payment method. A row the brand didn't report is left off, as on Order details today. The sample is a real Brandon Blackwood product at its real price; the order number is made up. On the iPhone, the stamp lands with a light haptic tap, and with Reduce Motion on, the receipt fades in and out instead of feeding and falling. Copy puts the receipt's text on the clipboard.

What's real: sizes, spacing and holiday colours come from the app's code (FloatingTabBar.swift, Navigation.swift, holidays.json). On the iPhone the search bar is iOS's own Liquid Glass, which bends light at the edges a little more than a browser can show.

Medium-tint tab bar (build 166, 10/24): you picked the "medium tint" from simulator renders. The same 62pt bar 20pt from the edges, its dark ink at 60% (the Instagram dark frost in builds 164-165 was 85%), so photos and names show through more as they scroll under it, with a light rim along the top edge. It is drawn as a blur rather than Liquid Glass, because Liquid Glass on the bar made taps on Cart and Browse miss in the UI tests; the search bar keeps real Liquid Glass. The selected tab gets a lighter frosted-grey pill; on a holiday the pill fills with the day's colours and drifts as before.
